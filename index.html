01 — The Case

You've earned
the customers.
Now own them.

Burger Mafia at Hoodi Main Road holds a 4.9 rating across 71 reviews. That is not a marketing problem — that is a kitchen doing its job exceptionally well. The problem is who currently owns the relationship with everyone who tastes it.

Today a hungry customer in Hoodi opens an aggregator app, and Burger Mafia appears beside twenty competitors, in their layout, under their search ranking, paying their commission on every order. You supply the food and the reputation. They keep the customer, the data and a cut of the bill.

This proposal is about changing that — while matching, feature for feature, the ordering platform of the biggest burger chain in the country.

18–25%Typical aggregator commission on every order they send you

WHY NOW 01

Commission compounds

Every order routed through an aggregator gives away a slice of a margin that is already thin in fast food. A direct order carries a payment fee of roughly two percent instead. The platform doesn't need to replace the aggregators — shifting even a modest share of repeat customers to direct ordering changes the economics of the outlet.

WHY NOW 02

You can't market to people you don't know

You cannot text a discount, launch a new burger to your regulars, or win back someone who stopped ordering, because you do not hold their number. Your own platform builds that list from the first order — and it belongs to you permanently.

WHY NOW 03

Your regulars are your cheapest growth

A 4.9 rating means people come back. A loyalty programme turns that goodwill into measurable repeat frequency — and on your own platform it costs you points, not commission.

We are not proposing you leave Zomato and Swiggy. Keep them for discovery — that is what they are good at. Own the second order, and every one after it.

FINDING 01

The page weighs more than the meal

Their storefront ships well over a megabyte of JavaScript before a single burger appears, and nothing renders until all of it arrives. On a mid-range phone on mobile data, that is the gap where a hungry customer gives up.

Opportunity
FINDING 02

The experience is an app in a browser

Dated layouts, cramped menu grids, modal-heavy flows and no real design language. Functional, but nothing about it makes you want to order. Food has to be sold visually.

Opportunity
FINDING 03

The restaurant floor is ignored

Delivery and takeaway only. A guest sitting at one of their tables gets nothing from the platform — no menu, no ordering, no loyalty accrual, no data captured.

Opportunity
FINDING 04

But the commercial engine is serious

A points programme, daily rewards, a coupon wallet, rider tipping, round-up charity donations, corporate discounts and per-item feedback capture. This is the part worth matching in full.

Match this
FINDING 05

Payments are built for India

UPI, cards, netbanking, wallets, pay-later, meal cards and cash on delivery — with proper reconciliation when a payment stalls mid-flow. Non-negotiable table stakes.

Match this

07 — Design & Brand

We build the
whole identity

The kitchen has a reputation; the brand doesn't yet have a system. There is no logo standard, no palette, no consistent visual system and nothing that looks the same twice across a menu board, a delivery box and a listing photo. We build the identity and the platform as one coherent piece of work.

DELIVERABLE 01

Brand identity

Name lockup and logo, colour palette, typography, tone of voice and a brand book your team can hold suppliers to. Extended to packaging, signage and in-store mockups.

DELIVERABLE 02

Food imagery

We use the photos and creatives you give us. If you don’t supply photos, we source royalty-free images from the internet and fit them to the brand. We do not run photo shoots.

DELIVERABLE 03

Design system

Every screen designed and signed off before a line of production code is written. Two creative directions to choose from, then one refined to completion — storefront and back office.

The look we're aiming for

Dark, confident and high contrast. Oversized display type, one loud accent colour, and food imagery given room to do the selling. Closer to a premium streetwear drop than a fast-food menu board.

How it should feel

Mobile first and thumb friendly, with motion used to give feedback rather than decorate. Items fly into a sticky basket; sheets slide up instead of modals blocking the view; everything holds a smooth frame rate on a mid-range Android phone.

11 — Straight Talk

Where this
could go wrong

We would rather raise these now than discover them together in week fourteen.

RISK 01

We become your till

With no existing point of sale, if our back office goes down, your counter stops taking orders. That is a serious responsibility and we treat it as one.

  • Kitchen screen keeps working through a network drop
  • Printed ticket fallback for the line
  • Monitoring and alerting from day one
  • A support commitment with defined response times
RISK 02

Stock images aren’t your food

Royalty-free images fill the gaps, but they won’t show your actual burgers. Your own photos sell better, so send what you have early — images can be swapped in the admin panel any time after launch.

RISK 03

The name is crowded

Other unrelated businesses already trade as Burger Mafia in Pune, Amritsar and Kerala. That is workable locally, but exclusive national rights to the name are unlikely, so we would not build a growth plan that depends on owning it. Worth a trademark search before we finalise the mark — and worth deciding now whether the identity leans on a distinctive lockup you can protect. Separately: we copy the incumbent's capability, never their name, logo or imagery.
